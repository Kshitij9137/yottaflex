# Model Report: Supportive Retention Signals

Synthetic data, seed `20261022`, `10,000` employees. Generated by `python -m yottaflex.model --data data/full --out data/full/model --report docs/model_report.md`.

## 1. Headline

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does it rank better than chance on a future period? | Yes, modestly. Test ROC-AUC 0.713 (95% CI 0.685-0.740), PR-AUC 0.094 (CI 0.079-0.116) against a base rate of 0.035 |
| What does a review team get? | Reviewing the top 5% of employees finds 20% of the people who later left voluntarily (4.1x better than random) |
| Is a more complex model better? | No. Gradient boosting scores PR-AUC 0.081 against 0.094 for the explainable model |
| Are leaks caught? | Yes. Production pipeline: 0 rows from after the cutoff. The leaky variants fail the audit (see section 5) |
| Is every score explained and reviewable? | Yes. A guard refuses to emit any row without a plain-language explanation, a reviewer slot, and the supportive framing |
| Any fairness concern? | Yes. The top-5% list over-represents long-tenure employees and under-represents first-year employees (section 7). Not fixed silently; a trade-off is shown |

## 2. What this is, and is not

> Supportive signal to prioritise a conversation. It is not an employment decision and must not be used for promotion, pay, performance or termination decisions.

- The output is a **priority list for supportive conversations** with HR partners. A person reviews every row.
- It is **not** a prediction that someone will resign, and never an input to pay, promotion, performance or termination decisions.
- Scores are relative priorities. With a 3-4% base rate and ROC-AUC near 0.7, most people on any list will not leave. The reasons shown are what to talk about, not conclusions about the person.

## 3. Temporal design

| Split | Cutoff (features as of) | Labels observed until | Scorable employees | Voluntary exits | Base rate | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Recall @5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 2025-03-31 | 2025-09-30 | 8,714 | 282 | 3.236% | 0.702 | 0.087 | 18.4% |
| validation | 2025-09-30 | 2026-03-31 | 8,826 | 289 | 3.274% | 0.708 | 0.092 | 22.1% |
| test | 2026-03-31 | 2026-09-30 | 8,937 | 315 | 3.525% | 0.713 | 0.094 | 20.3% |

- Train is in-sample, validation is out-of-sample for a train-only fit, **test is out-of-sample for a model fitted on train + validation**. Validation labels are fully observed before the test cutoff.
- Regularisation `C=0.01` was chosen on validation PR-AUC (0.01: 0.0919, 0.1: 0.0913, 1.0: 0.0917). The differences are tiny, so it is not a sensitive choice.
- Scorable means employed with a snapshot at the cutoff. Voluntary exit within 6 months is the label. Involuntary exits in the horizon are excluded (competing event). Labels are **recomputed** from termination facts, never read from the stored column.
- Split-rule audit: PASS. each horizon ends on or before the next cutoff, so training labels are observed before the test cutoff.

## 4. Features and results

21 features, all computed as of the cutoff from rows recorded by the end of that day: engagement (latest, drop, 4-quarter mean), workload (index, high-load quarters, consecutive **non-peak** overload), career (months since promotion, stagnation beyond 36 months), pay (ratio to band midpoint, gap below 95%), tenure, recent team change or promotion, and a recent retention conversation.
Not used: any sensitive attribute, job family, employment type, team or employee identifiers, any outcome field, and anything from the ground truth.
Missing data: a skipped survey is **missing**, never a score. No survey yet is filled with a neutral 60 plus a flag. The count of skipped surveys is included only so the model can show it carries no negative signal.

## 5. Leak audit and the leak ladder

The audit checks, on the feature table: (1) **provenance**, meaning the latest `recorded_at` and date among the rows each feature used, which must not exceed the cutoff; (2) no forbidden or sensitive column names; (3) no single feature that separates the outcome with AUC >= 0.90; (4) the split rule; (5) stored labels against recomputed labels. The ladder switches one defence off at a time, on the injected data.

| Pipeline | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | Recall @5% | Rows from after the cutoff | Audit | What caught it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reference: same pipeline on the clean baseline (no injected anomalies) | 0.713 | 0.095 | 20.0% | 0 | PASS |  |
| Production: as-of, recorded_at enforced, skipped = null, recomputed labels | 0.713 | 0.094 | 20.3% | 0 | PASS |  |
| Ignore recorded_at (date-only as-of) | 0.727 | 0.125 | 21.9% | 25 | FAIL |  |
| Skipped survey scored as its stored value (no sanitising) | 0.707 | 0.090 | 17.8% | 0 | PASS (not a leak) | caught by validator rule SURV-SKIPPED-HAS-SCORE (377 rows) |
| LEAK: latest-value join over all rows | 0.701 | 0.084 | 10.5% | 8797 | FAIL |  |
| LEAK: latest-value join + count of later snapshots | 0.990 | 0.953 | 98.1% | 8797 | FAIL | future_snapshots |
| Use the stored test label instead of recomputing it | 0.695 | 0.086 | 19.1% | 0 | FAIL | 55 labels disagree with termination facts |

What this shows:

1. **A small leak can inflate the metric visibly.** Ignoring `recorded_at` touches only 25 employees, whose revised rows lift them by +0.21 in score percentile, yet PR-AUC moves from 0.094 to 0.125. Date filtering alone is not enough; availability time matters.
2. **A leak can also hide.** The latest-value join does not raise the metric (PR-AUC 0.084); leavers' latest rows are near the cutoff and stayers' are newer. A metric-only check would pass it. The provenance audit flags 8,797 rows.
3. **A blatant leak is obvious.** Adding a count of later snapshots gives PR-AUC 0.953. A result that good is a warning, and the univariate check names the feature.
4. **Data-quality faults are a different layer.** Scoring skipped surveys as zero or trusting a corrupted stored label hurts the metric without any leak. They are caught by the validator (`SURV-SKIPPED-HAS-SCORE`, `RECON-LABEL`), and the production pipeline never depends on either.

## 6. Explanations and the output contract

Each employee's score is a sum of per-feature contributions to the log-odds relative to the average employee (`coefficient x standardised value`). Features are grouped into plain themes, the top three positive themes are shown with the employee's actual numbers, and survey participation is never listed as a reason.

- **Rank 1, score 0.332.** 1) Latest engagement score is 30/100 (average 68), down 10 points since the previous survey; 2) 122 months since the last promotion; 3) Pay is 89% of the band midpoint. Context: Planned workload peak this quarter (temporary, not counted as overload). Suggested topic: Check in on how work feels right now and what would help.
- **Rank 2, score 0.325.** 1) Latest engagement score is 40/100 (average 68), down 5 points since the previous survey; 2) 73 months since the last promotion; 3) Pay is 82% of the band midpoint. Suggested topic: Check in on how work feels right now and what would help.
- **Rank 3, score 0.316.** 1) Latest engagement score is 20/100 (average 68), down 20 points since the previous survey; 2) Pay is 80% of the band midpoint. Context: Moved teams recently. Suggested topic: Check in on how work feels right now and what would help.

`retention_signals.csv` holds the top 5% (447 employees) of the scorable population, the review capacity. Every row carries `human_review_required = True`, `automated_action_allowed = False`, an assigned HR partner, a manager, a `Pending_Review` status with an empty `reviewed_by_employee_id`, the framing note, and a non-empty explanation. `assert_supportive_framing` raises if any row breaks that contract, including decision-style wording such as terminate, dismiss or demote.

This is the model-side counterpart to the validator's `unreviewed_individual_score` detector: a score without an explanation or a reviewer cannot be produced.

## 7. Sensitive attributes, proxies and subgroup check

- No sensitive attribute exists in the data or the features (age, gender, ethnicity, caste, religion, nationality, disability, marital or family status, leave type, home location). A name audit confirms no feature column matches. Job family, employment type, team and level-independent identifiers are kept out of the model as well.
- Tenure, months since promotion and level are job-related and explainable, but in real data they would correlate with age. They are treated as **proxy risks** and monitored below.

**Finding.** Tenure bands are flagged very unevenly: 7+ years at 1.96x their share although their attrition is 1.03x the average, and under 1 year at 0.49x although theirs is 1.15x. Same review capacity, two ways of spending it:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global top 5% by score (default) | 447 | 20.3% | 14.3% | 4.06x | 0.26-1.96 |

Spending capacity in proportion to each tenure band evens out who is reviewed at a measurable cost in recall. This is a policy choice for HR and legal, so the default stays global ranking with the monitoring above. The fixed 0.8-1.25 band is a screening rule, not a legal test.

## 8. Limitations

- **Synthetic data.** Performance reflects the generating rule plus noise and says nothing about real attrition. The pipeline, audits and contract are the deliverable.
- **The rule is also the ceiling.** The model is near the transparent rule because that rule produced the labels. A real system would not have this reference.
- **Interventions.** Conversations lower simulated risk, so a good intervention process makes a model look worse over time. The model treats a recent conversation as a feature, not as causal evidence.
- **Leak audit scope.** It checks timestamps and feature provenance inside this dataset. A falsified `recorded_at` would need lineage controls outside it.
- **Fairness scope.** The subgroup check uses job-related groups because no sensitive attribute exists. With real data the same table should be built on protected groups, under legal guidance.
- **Single test period.** One test cutoff with 315 exits gives wide intervals (shown above); differences of a few points between models are not meaningful.
